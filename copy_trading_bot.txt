## What Is Copy Trading?

Copy trading means automatically mirroring someone else's trades — when they buy, you buy; when they sell, you sell. The idea is to piggyback on the research and insider knowledge of more informed traders.

---

## Why Politicians?

The transcript makes a compelling case for why US politicians are particularly interesting to copy:

- Members of Congress are **required by law to report their stock trades** publicly
- They sit on committees, regulate entire industries, and get briefed on policy changes **before the public hears about them**
- They know which companies are about to get government contracts and which ones may face investigation
- The data shows many of them **consistently beat the market**

The transcript backs this up with a backtest simulation:

| Strategy | Starting Capital | End Value | Return |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 (buy & hold) | $50,000 | $57,750 | ~15% |
| Copying politician trades | $50,000 | $67,400 | ~34.8% |

That's roughly a **2.2x return over the S&P 500** by simply copying one politician's trades.

## The Data Source: Capital Trades

The service used in the video is **Capital Trades** (a free, publicly accessible website). It aggregates and organizes congressional stock trade disclosures in one place, making it easy to see:

- Which politician bought or sold what
- When the trade was made

## Key Considerations & Limitations

### Disclosure Delay
This is the biggest caveat. Politicians are not required to disclose trades *immediately* — there can be a delay of **30 to 45 days** (or sometimes longer). The transcript acknowledges this but notes that most congressional trades tend to be longer-term positions (options contracts two or more years out), meaning even a delayed copy can still capture most of the gain.

### It's Still Paper Trading
Everything demonstrated in the video uses **fake money**. Real-world results with actual capital could differ significantly.

### Legality
Copying publicly disclosed trades is **legal** — the data is public record. However, acting on non-public information yourself would be insider trading. The key distinction here is that you're using *officially reported* disclosures, not private tips.

### Data Accuracy
Capital Trades relies on politicians self-reporting. There can be errors, omissions, or amended filings that could affect the accuracy of what the bot copies.

### No Guarantee of Future Performance
The backtest simulation showing 34.8% returns is based on historical data. Past performance of any politician's trading record does not guarantee future results.
